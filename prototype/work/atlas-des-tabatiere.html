Atlas des Tabatiere

A catalogue of the tobacco tins my partner’s grandfather nailed to the wall beside his fireplace, and of everything their lids tell about him.

The wall

My partner’s grandfather was an architect and an artist of everyday life. He was also a dedicated pipe smoker, and whenever a tin of tobacco was empty, he decorated its lid with a motif that interested him at the time and nailed it to the wall next to the fireplace in the family’s farmhouse. Over the years, a whole wall of them came together.

The catalogue

Years after his death, these small tins tell a story about him, and I wanted to make that story readable. I photographed every single tin, cut each one out from its background and brought them together in a catalogue. Then I researched the background of every image: where a motif came from, who made it, and what it might have meant to him at the time.

Why an atlas

The more lids I traced back, the clearer the clusters became: the old Surrealists, the Bauhaus, the early days of photography. Le Corbusier hangs right next to an old Dentogène advert. Read together, the lids map one man’s curiosity, which is why the catalogue became an atlas.
